THE METAPHYSICS OF CODE - SUBSTANCE VS ACCIDENT

SUBSTANCE VS ACCIDENT

Home Page

I. The Core Distinction

Every system has substance and accidents.

Substance is what the system communicates. It is the idea, the solution, the message delivered to the user.

Accidents are the machinery that supports the communication. They are the materials, the code, the wiring, the infrastructure.

The distinction is not absolute. It depends on the reference frame. What is substance in one frame may be accident in another.

II. What Is Substance?

Substance is the communicated solution within a given reference frame.

It is what the user perceives, understands, and relies on to achieve their goal.

In the elevator frame:

  • The buttons communicate direction.
  • The doors communicate opening and closing.
  • The floor indicator communicates location.
  • The motion communicates movement.
  • The chime communicates arrival.

These are substance. They are part of the solution. They are the communicated idea.

In the software frame:

  • The behavior of the code is substance.
  • The actions the user can take are substance.
  • The responses the user receives are substance.

The substance is what the software does. It is the meaning.

III. What Are Accidents?

Accidents are the machinery that supports the substance.

They are not part of the communicated solution. They are part of the implementation.

In the elevator frame:

  • The metal walls are accidents. They could be wood, glass, or plastic. The elevator remains an elevator.
  • The color of the buttons is accident. They could be red, blue, or green. The solution remains the same.
  • The wiring behind the buttons is accident. It could be copper, aluminum, or wireless. The solution remains the same.
  • The cables that lift the elevator are accidents. They could be different. The solution remains the same.

In the software frame:

  • The code is accident. It could be rewritten in a different language. The software remains the same.
  • The framework is accident. It could be swapped. The software remains the same.
  • The database is accident. It could be replaced. The software remains the same.
  • The deployment platform is accident. It could be moved. The software remains the same.

IV. The Criterion

The criterion for distinguishing substance from accident is:

If it is part of the communicated solution, it is substance.

If it is just machinery supporting that solution, it is accident.

The user can point to both. But only one is the message. The other is the medium.

  • You can point to the button. It is substance.
  • You can point to the metal wall. It is accident.
  • You can point to the floor indicator. It is substance.
  • You can point to the cable. It is accident.

Perception is the ground. But perception alone does not identify substance. You must ask: "Is this part of the solution, or is it just support?"

V. Reference Frames

Substance and accident are relative to the reference frame.

In the elevator frame:

  • The elevator is substance. It is the solution to moving between floors.
  • The buttons, doors, and indicators are substance. They communicate the solution.
  • The metal walls, cables, and wiring are accidents. They support the solution.

In the frame of "getting people between floors":

  • The elevator is an accident. It is one implementation among many.
  • Stairs are also an accident. Escalators are also an accident.
  • The substance is the resolution: "The person moves between floors."

In the software frame:

  • The software is substance. It is the solution.
  • The code is accident. It supports the solution.

In the frame of the problem being solved:

  • The software is accident. It is one implementation among many.
  • Human bureaucracy is also an accident. Mechanical systems are also an accident.
  • The substance is the resolution: "The problem is solved."

VI. The Ground

The ground is the perceivable problem.

  • I am hungry. I need to not be hungry.
  • I need to get to the fifth floor. I need to move.
  • I need to process data. I need the output.

The ground is the problem as it is felt, experienced, and perceived.

The substance in the ground frame is the resolution of that problem.

The accidents are all the implementations.

VII. The Humility of Software

Software is not special.

In its own frame, software is substance. It is the solution. It communicates meaning.

In a higher frame, software is accident. It is machinery. It is implementation. It is one way of solving a problem among many.

When you are building software, treat it as substance. Protect it. Ground it in perception.

When you are solving a problem, treat software as accident. Do not mistake it for the resolution. Keep it humble.

VIII. The Ideal Solution

The ideal solution is the accident that is necessary and sufficient—the smallest possible machinery that delivers the substance.

  • Not an elevator with metal walls and complex wiring.
  • Not a software system with frameworks and databases.
  • Just the solution. Just the communication. Just the substance.

The ideal is the accident that preserves the substance without adding unnecessary machinery.

IX. Summary

  • Substance is the communicated solution within a reference frame.
  • Accidents are the machinery that supports the solution.
  • The criterion: If it is part of the solution, it is substance. If it is just support, it is accident.
  • Reference frames: What is substance in one frame may be accident in another.
  • The ground: The perceivable problem.
  • The humility: Software is substance in its own frame, accident in a higher frame.
  • The ideal: The necessary and sufficient accident.

The Truth

The ground is perception. The substance is the solution. The accidents are the machinery.

Software is not special. It is substance when we build it. It is accident when we serve it.
