The Script of Freedom

Why Modern Liberties are a Hollywood Hoax

The lights are dim. The atmosphere is tense. A rich tech billionaire stands on stage. Let's call him Pavel. He speaks into the microphone. He uses a big metaphor.

"We are on the Titanic," Pavel warns the crowd. He looks very serious. "Our privacy and our Western freedom are heading toward an iceberg. The water is rushing into the cabins. People do not notice it. We must protect this ship."

The crowd listens carefully. It is a dramatic performance.

"CUT!"

A voice shouts from the darkness. The director walks onto the stage. He looks at Pavel. Then he looks at the fake plastic ship.

"I do not believe you," the director says. He waves his hand. "Stop the scene. It is hard to believe you are the good guy, Pavel. You run a multi-billion-dollar platform. You and your friends at Google claim to protect our freedom. But everything changes when politics change. You flipped a switch after the war broke out. You locked out millions of ordinary Russian users overnight. They lost their digital lives for a war they did not start. You do not save the ship. You own the locks."

Pavel stands there in silence. His illusion is broken.

Let's leave Pavel on his stage. We can walk out of this room. We step over the cables on the concrete floor. We push through heavy doors into the next studio room. We want to see what is really happening.

We walk around to the back of the set. We look behind the big "Iceberg" prop. From the front, it looks like a scary threat to human rights. It looks like censorship and government control. But the back is just plywood and scaffolding. There is no monster here.

Instead, Mickey Mouse sits on a soft velvet sofa behind the drywall. He is casually smoking weed.

It is an absurd picture. There is no noble crusade behind the drama of "global liberty." There is only a childish desire for comfort. People just want convenience and the status quo. The West turned freedom into a lifestyle brand. They sell it with Hollywood movies, Coca-Cola, jeans, and pop music. We think liberty means choosing between thirty brands of soda. We think it means hiding our data from the government.

We are terrified of losing the wrong things. We worry about losing our comfort. We want the freedom to stay passive. Meanwhile, our real human power is dying.

The real iceberg is not the government taking our data. The real iceberg is our addiction to comfort. The status quo tricks us. It makes us stop making real choices.

Politics is a huge theatrical production. But it is a very weak tool for solving human problems. Modern politics views freedom as an external system. People think the system must make life easy. If someone succeeds in the West, people think it is normal. If someone succeeds in another country, people act like they beat impossible odds.

But reality does not care about these labels. True freedom does not depend on your environment. It is not a legal paper from a government. It is not an app from a billionaire.

True freedom lives inside the person. A human being can become anything they want. Circumstances do not stop them.

This is where true liberty connects with Christianity. It is a total rejection of the world's theater. Give to Caesar what is Caesar’s. Give to God what is God’s. Let the politicians have their stage. Let the tech elites believe their own lies.

Your path belongs to you. Your search for truth belongs to you. You are the source of your own journey. True freedom is an internal choice. You choose to seek the truth and do what is right. It does not matter if the sinking ship keeps you comfortable or throws you away.
