Imagine a person who becomes entirely obsessed with a single, highly visible metric of physical fitness: the abdominal muscles. Day after day, year after year, they pour every ounce of their energy into training their core. They ignore their legs, their shoulders, and, crucially, their back.
When they look in the mirror, they see immediate, intoxicating results. Six-pack, eight-pack—a marvel of hyper-focused optimization. They smile at the reflection, convinced they are achieving the pinnacle of health, and the media reinforces it, telling them this is exactly what a successful body looks like.
But behind their back, a silent horror is unfolding. Because the front of the body is pulling with immense, unnatural force while the back remains entirely neglected, the spine begins to twist. The structural integrity of the entire organism is compromised. Eventually, this person ends up with a severe, irreversible case of scoliosis. The very thing they optimized for ends up making their daily life miserable, agonizing, and fundamentally broken.
This is not just a lesson in weightlifting. It is a fundamental law of existence. Whatever causes profound imbalance is the thing we really need to fear, and it is the thing we must fix as soon as possible. The greater the imbalance, the worse the ultimate catastrophe—even if, in the short term, it looks completely fine, or even highly desirable, to a blindsided world.

---

The Mirror and the Illusion of the Ultra-Success
We are a culture addicted to looking in the mirror and praising the overdeveloped muscle. From an early age, our media saturates us with images of the ultra-rich, the ultra-successful, and the ultra-beautiful. Celebrities and corporate titans are meticulously designed to be likable. They capture our attention, and in doing so, our collective attention acts as a massive feedback loop, amplifying their wealth and status to astronomical heights.
We look at one individual possessing a trillion dollars of wealth, or a single hyper-dense city like Hong Kong concentrated with massive population and capital, and society calls it a marvel of achievement. We look at the mirror and say, "Let’s keep going. More growth, more optimization."
But if you look away from the reflection and examine the hidden spine of our world, this extreme imbalance is the root source of our deepest horrors:
• On a human scale: Millions of children grow up hating their own reflections, trapped in a state of permanent psychological misery because they are constantly comparing themselves to a localized, unnatural ideal of beauty.
• On an individual scale: The very celebrities we envy get access to every vice imaginable, becoming hopelessly addicted, turning into "slaves" to their own hyper-optimized public personas, and sometimes destroying themselves entirely.
• On a systemic scale: The ultra-wealthy engage in increasingly predatory, complex tricks to maintain their positions. The higher the stakes, the greater the collateral harm inflicted on the rest of the human ecosystem.
Like the bodybuilder ignoring his spine, we mistake localized, aggressive growth for overall health, completely oblivious to the structural damage pulling our societal spine out of alignment.

---

The Steroid Era: AI and Exponential Ruin
This structural twisting is dangerous enough on its own, but we have recently introduced an element that removes all natural limits: exponential technology.
In the realm of Information Technology, and now most acutely with Artificial Intelligence, products don't just scale—they accelerate. Success breeds the exact tools needed to achieve the next layer of success at a fraction of the time. The wealth, power, and capability generated by AI are fed directly back into making the AI smarter and faster, creating a runaway feedback loop of unprecedented power.
If traditional optimization is a flawed workout routine, AI is the introduction of massive doses of synthetic steroids.
Steroids give you quick, amazing, jaw-dropping results in the mirror. They make the overdeveloped muscle grow at a thousand times its natural pacing. But underneath the surface, they drastically worsen the underlying crisis. They accelerate the structural collapse, pushing the spine to its breaking point far before the body has time to organically adapt or signal that something is deeply wrong. By the time the pain becomes unbearable, the damage is already absolute.

---

The Remedy: The Spiritual Full-Body Workout
How do we stop a runaway system when our primal instincts are hardwired to keep chasing more? Instincts alone will always propel us toward bigger gains, higher optimization, and sharper imbalances. They tell us to keep training the muscle that is already winning.
Only the mind and the spirit can counteract this trajectory.
To possess a mind—to be a truly conscious being—means your fundamental duty is to step back from the mirror, observe the whole system, and actively apply the brakes. If you see that something has become immensely, overwhelmingly popular, your job is to do the opposite. You must deliberately choose to be the disliker, the counter-weight, the friction in the machine.
This requires a radical, deeply idealistic shift in how we handle concentrated power:
If you find yourself sitting on billions of dollars, you must realize that you are not sitting on a throne—you are sitting on a timebomb of systemic imbalance. The spiritual mandate is to actively put those billions to use causing as much "braking" to other hyper-optimized businesses (including your own) as possible. The goal must be to deliberately dilute that concentrated mass, to willingly lose that distorting power, so that the system can breathe again.
We must commit to a philosophical full-body workout. The moment you notice one part of yourself, your culture, or your economy developing at an aggressive, runaway pace, you must oppose it in your daily practice as hard as you can. We must intentionally cultivate the neglected corners of our spirit, our communities, and our lives.
We must stop worshiping the distorted view in the glass, turn around, and start fixing our twisted spine before it breaks us completely.
