I have read widely in the history of philosophy — Plato, Aristotle, Aquinas, Descartes and others — but the framework I write from rests mainly on two books: Berkeley's _Principles of Human Knowledge_ and Kant's _Critique of Pure Reason_. Recently I added Schopenhauer's _On the Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason_ and Ernst Mach.

A framework that rests on a few pillars has a specific weakness, however widely its author has read. I can't always tell which of my conclusions are discoveries and which are rediscoveries. And I am most likely to miss the strongest arguments against my own starting points, because they come from thinkers who took the same tools further, or in another direction, than I did.

So this list has three purposes:

1. **The critics.** Thinkers who used my own tools — the same razor I take from Berkeley — against my conclusions.
2. **The ancestors.** Thinkers who reached my ideas before me, so I can cite and build on them instead of rederiving them.
3. **The traditions I make claims about.** Buddhism, Chinese thought, physics: I write about all of them, so I should read them at the source.

Some entries are books I have read before, for other reasons. They are here because I now need them to answer a specific question. Each entry says where it touches something I have written. Entries marked ★ come first.

## Notes on my recent reading

**Schopenhauer agrees with my mechanism but not my conclusion.** In _The World as Will and Representation_, the will never reaches rest. Satisfaction is only a short break from pain, and life "swings like a pendulum" between pain and boredom (§57). That is nearly my essay [Why Intelligence Can Never Rest](./intelligence-indirection.html). But his way out is to deny the will — the same dissolution of the self I reject in Buddhism. Our sharpest disagreement is in Book 4, not in the _Fourfold Root_.

**Mach is not just a weaker Berkeley.** He is Berkeley without the perceiving spirit and without God. His "elements" are neither mental nor physical, and he writes that "the ego cannot be saved." That is Hume's move, and it is the biggest threat to my foundation: if Berkeley's razor removes material substance, what stops it from removing mental substance too?

**Hegel deserves a fairer target.** "Thesis–antithesis–synthesis" is Fichte's vocabulary, popularized by Chalybäus. Hegel rarely used it, and in the Preface to the _Phenomenology_ he dismisses formulaic triads as a "lifeless schema." [Chapter 4](./the-only-thing-constant-is-change.01.04.html) of my book credits him with the triad. I may still reject Hegel, but the critique should hit what he actually wrote.

## 1. The critics of my foundation

- [ ] ★ **David Hume** — _A Treatise of Human Nature_, 1.4.6 "Of personal identity," and _An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding_, §7 "Of the idea of necessary connexion." He turns Berkeley's razor on the self and on causal necessity. That challenges both my anchor in the certainty of the "I" and my claim that "every cause is a conflict" is known a priori.

- [ ] ★ **George Berkeley** — _Three Dialogues between Hylas and Philonous_, the third dialogue. Hylas raises exactly the objection above, and Philonous answers that we have a "notion" of spirit. I need to decide whether that answer satisfies me.

- [ ] ★ **Immanuel Kant** — the _Paralogisms of Pure Reason_, the _Refutation of Idealism_ (B274), and the _Prolegomena_, §13. Kant's direct reply to Berkeley by name, and to "the I is the one certain substance." I use Kant against the noumenon; here he uses himself against me.

- [ ] **Plato** — _Theaetetus_. The original examination of "knowledge is perception," and Socrates's case against it.

- [ ] **Friedrich Nietzsche** — _Beyond Good and Evil_, Part 1, especially §16–17. "A thought comes when 'it' wants": the "I" in "I think" as a habit of grammar. My own anti-reification tool, pointed at my cogito.

- [ ] **G. W. F. Hegel** — _Phenomenology of Spirit_, the Preface and chapter 1, "Sense-Certainty." He argues that bare "this, here, now" perception is the emptiest kind of knowledge. The fairest test of whether Hegel is sophistry.

- [ ] **Wilfrid Sellars** — "Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind," in the edition with Robert Brandom's study guide. The "Myth of the Given": perception can't ground knowledge unless it is already conceptual.

- [ ] ★ **W. V. O. Quine** — "On What There Is" and "Two Dogmas of Empiricism." The first is the standard modern account of when a theory is committed to an entity — my "ghost" question made formal. The second attacks the analytic/synthetic distinction my a priori/a posteriori split depends on.

- [ ] ★ **Ludwig Wittgenstein** — _Philosophical Investigations_, §65–67 (family resemblance as an alternative to essence) and §243–315 (the private language argument). The deepest challenge to essence-seeking and to building meaning out of private experience.

## 2. The ancestors of my ideas

- [ ] **Heraclitus and Parmenides** — the fragments, in Robin Waterfield's _The First Philosophers_. The title of my book paraphrases Heraclitus; Parmenides argued that change is unreal.

- [ ] **Aristotle** — _Physics_ I–III, _Categories_ 1–5, and _Metaphysics_ VII. "The rule never stalls" in [What Are Prime Numbers?](./philosophy-of-math.primes.html) is his potential infinity. _Categories_ read against _Metaphysics_ VII also untangles substance and essence, two words I use in opposite senses in different essays.

- [ ] ★ **Augustine** — _Confessions_, I.1 and Book XI. "Our heart is restless until it rests in You" is [Chapter 5](./the-only-thing-constant-is-change.02.05.html) in one sentence. Book XI calls time a "distension of the mind" — that is Chapter 4.

- [ ] **Al-Ghazali** — _The Incoherence of the Philosophers_ (the 17th discussion) and _Deliverance from Error_. Fire doesn't cause cotton to burn; God coordinates the two events. That is my brain/mind coordination, six centuries before Berkeley. _Deliverance_ moves from crisis to doubt to faith, as my book does.

- [ ] **Nicolas Malebranche** — _Dialogues on Metaphysics and on Religion_. Occasionalism, and Berkeley's immediate source.

- [ ] **G. W. Leibniz** — _Monadology_. Where the principle of sufficient reason starts; his pre-established harmony is my VR-engine image.

- [ ] **Baruch Spinoza** — _Ethics_, Parts II–III. His conatus says everything strives to _persist_ — the opposite of "everything wills rest." His parallelism of mind and body is the "two frames of reference" in my [Manifesto of Decoupled Science](./decoupled-science-manifesto.html).

- [ ] ★ **Arthur Schopenhauer** — _The World as Will and Representation_, vol. 1, Book 4, and the appendix "Criticism of the Kantian Philosophy." The continuation of the _Fourfold Root_, and the place where my disagreement with him is sharpest (see the note above).

- [ ] **Sigmund Freud** — _Beyond the Pleasure Principle_. The death drive as an urge to return to an earlier, inorganic rest. With it I can place myself among four positions on what everything wants:
  - to persist (Spinoza);
  - to strive without end (Schopenhauer);
  - to return to rest (Aristotle's natural places, Freud);
  - to rest only in God (Augustine — and me).

- [ ] **Blaise Pascal** — _Pensées_, the wager fragment. My "0% versus ε" argument in [Change](./change.html). I need to know the many-gods objection; my comparison of Christianity and Buddhism is in effect my answer to it.

- [ ] **Charles Sanders Peirce** — "The Fixation of Belief" and "How to Make Our Ideas Clear." The pragmatic maxim is my grounding test, published in 1878.

- [ ] **Alfred North Whitehead** — _Science and the Modern World_, chapter 3, and _The Concept of Nature_, chapter 2. His "fallacy of misplaced concreteness" is my reification; his "bifurcation of nature" is my critique of materialism.

- [ ] **Henri Bergson** — "An Introduction to Metaphysics" and _Time and Free Will_. The main philosopher of change: lived duration comes before time treated as space.

## 3. Natural philosophy: Newton, Einstein, and their debates

- [ ] **Galileo Galilei** — _The Assayer_ (excerpts). Colors and tastes "reside only in consciousness," and the book of nature is "written in the language of mathematics." Where the split I reject begins.

- [ ] **Isaac Newton** — _Principia_: the Scholium on absolute space and time, the "Rules of Reasoning in Philosophy" (Book III), and the General Scholium. Absolute space and time as containers are what Chapter 4 opposes. Rule 1, "admit no more causes than are true and sufficient," is my anti-ghost principle. "Hypotheses non fingo" is my "science describes how, not why."

- [ ] ★ **Leibniz and Samuel Clarke** — _The Leibniz–Clarke Correspondence_. Leibniz argues space and time are relations; Clarke, speaking for Newton, defends absolute space as God's "sensorium." The classic debate behind my claim that space and time are relational.

- [ ] **George Berkeley** — _De Motu_, and _Principles_, §110–117. Berkeley against Newton: forces are mathematical tools, not real entities — my "force is a label" in [Change](./change.html).

- [ ] **Ernst Mach** — _The Science of Mechanics_, chapter 2, the section on Newton's views of time, space and motion. Mach's strongest book; his critique of Newton's bucket argument was a starting point Einstein credited.

- [ ] ★ **Albert Einstein** — "On the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies" (1905), §1, and _Relativity: The Special and the General Theory_, chapter 8. He gets rid of absolute simultaneity by asking which observation, with clocks and light signals, it could correspond to. The most successful case of grounding a concept in perception in the history of physics.

- [ ] ★ **Albert Einstein** — "Geometry and Experience" and "Autobiographical Notes" (in Schilpp, ed., _Albert Einstein: Philosopher-Scientist_). "As far as the laws of mathematics refer to reality, they are not certain" bears on my a priori/a posteriori split. The _Notes_ credit Hume and Mach, then explain why he became a realist.

- [ ] **Werner Heisenberg** — _Physics and Beyond_, the chapter on his 1926 conversation with Einstein. Heisenberg built quantum mechanics from observable quantities only — my rule. Einstein answered that "it is the theory which decides what we can observe." The strongest objection to perception-first thinking ever made by a physicist.

- [ ] **Niels Bohr** — "Discussion with Einstein on Epistemological Problems in Atomic Physics" (also in the Schilpp volume). The realism debate at its peak.

- [ ] **Henri Poincaré** — _Science and Hypothesis_. Geometry as convention: support for "mathematics is rules," and an influence on Einstein.

## 4. Philosophy of science

- [ ] **Pierre Duhem** — _The Aim and Structure of Physical Theory_. A working physicist arguing that physics describes but does not explain — [Chapter 6](./the-only-thing-constant-is-change.02.06.html).

- [ ] **Karl Popper** — _Conjectures and Refutations_, chapters 1, 3 and 6. Chapter 6 is "A Note on Berkeley as Precursor of Mach and Einstein." Chapter 1 is my [critique of Domain-Driven Design](./meataphysics-of-code.intro.html): unfalsifiable theories explaining away every failure.

- [ ] **Thomas Kuhn** — _The Structure of Scientific Revolutions_. [The Illusion of Progress](./the-illusion-of-progress.html) in mature form.

- [ ] **Imre Lakatos** — "Falsification and the Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes." How a theory's protected core survives failure — the "it wasn't done correctly" defense.

- [ ] **Rudolf Carnap** — _The Logical Structure of the World_ (the _Aufbau_). The most serious attempt to build everything from elementary experiences — a real test of my claim that grounding in perception is sufficient.

- [ ] **Nelson Goodman** — _The Structure of Appearance_. Why the _Aufbau_ stalled. The most useful lesson in this list for Empirical Software.

- [ ] ★ **Bas van Fraassen** — _The Scientific Image_ and _The Empirical Stance_. The best modern defense of my view on atoms: accept theories as empirically adequate, stay agnostic about what can't be observed.

- [ ] **David Lindley** — _Boltzmann's Atom_. The Mach–Boltzmann dispute over atoms is the historical test of "if you can't perceive it, it's a ghost," and Mach is usually judged to have lost.

## 5. Mind and consciousness

- [ ] **Edmund Husserl** — _Cartesian Meditations_, then _The Crisis of European Sciences_. Phenomenology is a disciplined version of "begin with what is given to consciousness." The _Crisis_ blames Galileo for hiding the lived world, close to my science manifesto.

- [ ] **Thomas Nagel** — "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"

- [ ] **David Chalmers** — "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness." The source of the "hard problem" I keep citing.

- [ ] **Daniel Dennett** — "Quining Qualia." The strongest materialist reply, to be read as the best case against me.

- [ ] **John Foster** — _A World for Us: The Case for Phenomenalistic Idealism_. A rigorous modern defense of Berkeley-style idealism with God ordering experience. The closest ally I know of.

## 6. Ethics and the good life

- [ ] **Aristotle** — _Nicomachean Ethics_. The baseline account of the good life my [questionnaire](./questionaire.html) asks about.

- [ ] **Arthur Schopenhauer** — _On the Basis of Morality_. Compassion rather than duty as the foundation of ethics, argued against Kant. That is [Morality Is Not Make-Believe](./non-kantian-morality.html), and the natural next book after the _Fourfold Root_.

- [ ] **Mencius** — 《孟子·公孙丑上》, 2A6: the child at the well and the "four sprouts." Inborn compassion that can be grown or stunted — the same structure as my math-intuition analogy.

- [ ] **Søren Kierkegaard** — _The Sickness unto Death_. Despair and faith, from a fellow critic of Hegel's systems.

- [ ] **Fyodor Dostoevsky** — "The Grand Inquisitor," _The Brothers Karamazov_, Book V, chapter 5. People trading freedom for bread and comfort — [The Script of Freedom](./the-script-of-freedom.html).

## 7. Traditions I make claims about

- [ ] **Bhikkhu Bodhi, ed.** — _In the Buddha's Words_. I call Buddhism self-annihilation, but the Buddha explicitly rejected annihilationism as a wrong view. I should meet that before arguing against it.

- [ ] **Nāgārjuna** — _Mūlamadhyamakakārikā_. The most radical anti-reification thinker there is: nothing has inherent existence.

- [ ] **Vasubandhu** — _Twenty Verses_. Consciousness-only idealism, argued with the same dream argument I use.

- [ ] **Laozi** — 《道德经》, especially chapter 16: "归根曰静," returning to the root is called stillness.

- [ ] **Zhuangzi** — 《齐物论》, chapter 2, ending with the butterfly dream I cite in my questionnaire.

- [ ] **Gongsun Long** — 《白马论》, the "White Horse" dialogue. A debate about names and reification.

- [ ] **Zhu Xi and Lü Zuqian** — 《近思录》, _Reflections on Things at Hand_. Zhu Xi's 理 (principle) is a single unifying principle — a test for my claim in [The Architecture of Chaos](./the-architecture-of-chaos.html) that Chinese thought never looked for one.

- [ ] **Vladimir Solovyov** — «Кризис западной философии (против позитивистов)», _The Crisis of Western Philosophy: Against the Positivists_. A Christian critique of positivism from the Russian tradition.

## 8. Mathematics and language

- [ ] **Gottlob Frege** — _The Foundations of Arithmetic_. The strongest attack on empiricist views of number; my primes essay should survive it.

- [ ] **David Hilbert** — "On the Infinite." Mathematics as a game with rules, held to the finitary standpoint.

- [ ] **L. E. J. Brouwer** — "Intuitionism and Formalism." Mathematics as only what a process can construct. My primes essay sits between him and Hilbert.

- [ ] **George Lakoff** — _Women, Fire, and Dangerous Things_. Supports my view that classification is experiential, not taxonomic.

- [ ] **George Lakoff and Mark Johnson** — _Metaphors We Live By_. Argues that all abstract thought rests on metaphor — a direct challenge to [The Analogy Trap](./analogy-is-not-grounding.html).

- [ ] **Guy Deutscher** — _Through the Language Glass_. A careful, critical look at "language shapes thought," the thesis of my [ОНТОЛОГИЯ ЯЗЫКА](./language-ontology.html).

- [ ] **Umberto Eco** — _The Search for the Perfect Language_. The history of invented philosophical languages that [简单话](./simple-chinese-language.html) belongs to.

## 9. Philosophy of programming

The same rule applies here as with Hegel: I criticize these works often, so I should criticize what they actually say.

### The sources of what I critique

- [ ] ★ **Robert C. Martin** — "Design Principles and Design Patterns" (2000), then _Clean Architecture_. The paper is where the SOLID principles first appear together. In _Clean Architecture_ the Single Responsibility Principle becomes "responsible to one, and only one, actor" — the stakeholder definition I attack in [SOLID Is Aristotelian Metaphysics](./metaphysics-of-code.solid.html). The same book also argues "The Database Is a Detail" and "Frameworks Are Details," and its "Screaming Architecture" chapter is my [Decoupling Code from File Structure](./code-file-structure-decoupling.html). He is closer to me than I usually admit.

- [ ] **Robert C. Martin** — _Clean Code_. The book most of my profession learned "clean" from, and the one I call aesthetic rather than epistemic.

- [ ] **Bertrand Meyer** — _Object-Oriented Software Construction_, and **Barbara Liskov and Jeannette Wing** — "A Behavioral Notion of Subtyping." Where the Open-Closed and Liskov Substitution principles actually come from, before they were packed into an acronym.

- [ ] ★ **Eric Evans** — _Domain-Driven Design_: chapter 2 on the ubiquitous language, chapters 5–6 on entities, aggregates and repositories, and Part IV on bounded contexts. I call the domain "the organization's model of its own bureaucracy." This is the book that claim has to survive.

- [ ] **Martin Fowler** — _Patterns of Enterprise Application Architecture_. The origin of the Repository, the Service Layer and the Data Mapper — the "ghosts" in [The Labyrinth of Fake Entities](./meataphysics-of-code.subjectivity.html). Add his short essay "Anemic Domain Model": by its standard, my plain data and pure functions are an anti-pattern.

- [ ] **Gamma, Helm, Johnson and Vlissides** — _Design Patterns_, chapter 1. The zoo of named patterns I call reified, and the two principles from it I keep anyway: program to an interface, favor composition over inheritance.

- [ ] **Alan Kay** — "The Early History of Smalltalk." What object-oriented programming meant before it meant class hierarchies: messages, not things.

### Allies, ancestors and rivals

- [ ] ★ **Fred Brooks** — "No Silver Bullet" and "'No Silver Bullet' Refired," in _The Mythical Man-Month_ (anniversary edition). Essence and accident in software, at the source of my [Silver Bullet and Context Dependency](./no-silver-bullet.html).

- [ ] ★ **Ben Moseley and Peter Marks** — "Out of the Tar Pit." Essential versus accidental state, and derived data as accidental state. The closest existing statement of why my essence keeps one fact instead of a fact plus a cached count.

- [ ] **David Parnas** — "On the Criteria To Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules." Decompose by the decisions likely to change. The strongest rival to my rule of decomposing by what the user perceives.

- [ ] **Edsger W. Dijkstra** — "On the Role of Scientific Thought" (EWD447). Where "separation of concerns" was coined.

- [ ] **Peter Naur** — "Programming as Theory Building." The program is a theory in programmers' minds, not the source text — [Software Is Made of Ideas, Not Matter](./software-is-an-idea.html).

- [ ] **Harold Abelson and Gerald Jay Sussman** — _Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs_. "Programs must be written for people to read, and only incidentally for machines to execute." Abstraction barriers done honestly.

- [ ] **John Ousterhout** — _A Philosophy of Software Design_, and his published debate with Robert Martin. Deep modules, complexity as dependencies plus obscurity, and a direct argument against _Clean Code_'s small-function advice.

- [ ] **Alistair Cockburn** — "Hexagonal Architecture." Ports and adapters: the pure core with accidents at the edge, named in 2005.

- [ ] **Gary Bernhardt** — "Boundaries" (talk). "Functional core, imperative shell" — my [Rule of the Pure Core](./empirical-software-manifesto.html) in one phrase.

- [ ] **Mark Seemann and Steven van Deursen** — _Dependency Injection: Principles, Practices, and Patterns_. Where the "composition root" I build every app around comes from.

- [ ] **Rich Hickey** — "Simple Made Easy" and "Are We There Yet?" (talks). The first separates simple from easy; the second builds identity, state and time on Whitehead.

- [ ] **Kent Beck** — _Test-Driven Development: By Example_; **Martin Fowler** — _Refactoring_. The loop I use for every change to an essence, and the discipline that keeps software soft.

- [ ] **Christopher Alexander** — _Notes on the Synthesis of Form_ and _A Pattern Language_, with Richard Gabriel's _Patterns of Software_. Design as resolving conflicts between form and context — my conflict-resolution model, applied to design — and the architect whose "patterns" programmers borrowed and, by Gabriel's account, misunderstood.

- [ ] **Terry Winograd and Fernando Flores** — _Understanding Computers and Cognition_. Heidegger applied to software design, and the easiest way into Heidegger for me.

## Order of attack

If I only read ten, in this order:

1. Hume, _Treatise_ 1.4.6 and _Enquiry_ §7
2. Berkeley, _Three Dialogues_, the third dialogue
3. Kant, the _Paralogisms_ and the _Refutation of Idealism_
4. Schopenhauer, _The World as Will and Representation_, vol. 1, Book 4
5. Augustine, _Confessions_ I and XI
6. _The Leibniz–Clarke Correspondence_
7. Einstein, the 1905 paper §1, then "Autobiographical Notes"
8. Quine, "On What There Is" and "Two Dogmas of Empiricism"
9. Wittgenstein, _Philosophical Investigations_ §65–67 and §243–315
10. van Fraassen, _The Scientific Image_

The first three test my foundation. Schopenhauer and Augustine place my metaphysics of change among existing views. Leibniz–Clarke and Einstein test my claims about space, time and physics. The last three test my grounding criterion against its strongest modern critics.

For programming, four more, in this order: Brooks, "Out of the Tar Pit," Evans, and Martin's _Clean Architecture_. The first two are where my position comes from; the last two are what it argues against.

An understanding that has never met its strongest critics is not soft. It is only untested.
