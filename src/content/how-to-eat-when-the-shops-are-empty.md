Picture the day the shops do not open. The shelves are bare. No trucks come. Nobody knows for how long.

What do you eat tomorrow? And the day after? And in month three?

This article answers that with pictures you can hold in your head. It is for one person. Multiply by the number of mouths you feed.

## The picture: one battery and three small machines

Imagine your kitchen has four things on the table.

- **The battery: the pantry.** A sack of rice, a sack of beans, a bottle of oil. It keeps you alive. It only goes down, never up.
- **Machine 1: the jar.** Dry beans go in. Fresh green sprouts come out in 3 to 5 days. It gives you vitamins and fresh food.
- **Machine 2: the bag.** Wet straw or cardboard goes in, with a pinch of mushroom seed (spawn). Mushrooms come out in about 4 weeks. It turns something you cannot eat into something you can.
- **Machine 3: the bin.** Bran and vegetable peels go in, with some mealworms. More mealworms come out in about 2 months. They carry protein and fat.

Be honest about what this is. The battery gives the calories. The machines keep you healthy, add protein and fat, and stretch the battery. This is **not a farm**. If the crisis is longer than your pantry, you will need land, potatoes, animals or a community. Part 3 says how.

## Part 1: The numbers, as pictures

A grown person needs about **2,000 calories a day**. Here is that day in a kitchen:

- **One mug of dry rice** (about 700 calories).
- **One mug of dry beans or lentils** (about 700 calories).
- **One shot glass of oil, 4 spoons** (about 480 calories).

That is about 1,900 calories. Add sprouts and mushrooms for vitamins. The numbers are rounded, but the picture is right.

For **one month, one person**, that is:

- 30 mugs of rice, about **6 kg**.
- 30 mugs of beans, about **6 kg**.
- **2 litres** of oil.

For three months, multiply by three. It fits in two buckets and a bottle crate.

Three more things people forget:

- **Salt.** About 5 grams a day, about 2 kg for a year. Without it you feel weak and get cramps.
- **Water.** 2 to 3 litres a day to drink and cook. Boil it for a few minutes if you are not sure it is clean.
- **Fuel.** Cooking beans takes a lot of fuel. A pressure cooker uses far less. After the pot boils, wrap it in towels and blankets in a box for an hour. It keeps cooking by itself ("hay box").

## Part 2: Day by day

### Day 0: the day the shops stay closed

**Picture:** you stand in front of an empty shelf. Do not panic. Count.

**Do:**
1. Put all your rice, beans and oil on the table. Measure them in **mugs** and spoons. Write the numbers on paper.
2. Divide by the number of people. That is your **number of days**. Write it on the wall.
3. Fill every bottle and pot with water.
4. Start **jar 1**. Two spoons of dry mung beans (or lentils) into a jar. Cover with water. Leave overnight.

**Eat:** a normal dinner. Use what spoils first (fresh vegetables, bread, thawing meat).

### Day 1: eat what spoils first

**Picture:** a fridge slowly getting warm.

**Do:**
1. Cook all the meat and fish from the fridge and freezer today and tomorrow. Salt thin strips of the rest and dry them in the sun or by a window.
2. Jar 1: pour out the water, rinse, drain, lay the jar on its side with a cloth over the mouth. Do this **morning and evening**.
3. Set up the **bin**: a plastic box with a lid, a hand-sized hole in the lid covered with mesh. Fill it 3 cm deep with bran (or oats). Add the mealworms. Put a slice of potato or carrot on top. Keep it at room temperature.

**Eat:** the cooked meat with rice. Save the pantry.

### Day 2: set up the mushroom bag

**Picture:** a bucket of wet straw.

**Do:**
1. Cut straw (or torn cardboard, or coffee grounds) into pieces as long as your finger.
2. Soak in **hot (not boiling) water for 1 hour**. This kills most germs.
3. Drain it until a squeezed handful gives only a few drops.
4. Mix it with the spawn in a clean bag or bucket. Poke small holes in the sides. Close it. Put it somewhere **warm and dark**. Do not open it.

**Eat:** one mug of rice cooked as porridge with a spoon of oil and a pinch of salt.

### Day 3: the first sprouts

**Picture:** tiny white tails coming out of the beans.

**Do:**
1. Jar 1 is ready. Start **jar 2** today. Start a new jar every day for a week, so one is always ready.
2. **Cook your sprouts for 2 minutes**. In a crisis there are no doctors. Stomach sickness can kill you. Raw sprouts are a risk.

**Eat:**
- **Morning:** rice porridge, oil, salt.
- **Midday:** beans cooked in the pressure cooker, with a handful of sprouts stirred in at the end.
- **Evening:** rice with a spoon of oil and fried sprouts.

### Days 4 to 7: build the rhythm

**Picture:** a routine, like brushing your teeth.

- **Morning (10 minutes):** rinse the jars. Look at the bag (do not open). Add a peel to the bin.
- **Evening (10 minutes):** rinse the jars. Cook with the hay box.

On day 7, write down how much is left in the buckets. If it falls faster than your plan, **eat less today**, not tomorrow.

### Week 2: plant

**Picture:** a bucket with holes in the bottom, dirt inside, and a sprouted potato.

**Do:**
1. If you have potatoes or sweet potatoes that have started to sprout, plant them 10 cm deep in buckets or bags. Cover with earth as the green grows. You will harvest in **3 to 4 months**.
2. The bag: you should see white fuzz spreading through the straw. That is good. **Blue, green or black spots are mould.** Throw that bag out and start again.
3. The bin: you should see worms eating. Keep the bran dry and add a few peels.

**Eat:** the same plate as before. Boring is fine. Boring is alive.

### Week 3: wait

**Picture:** a white brick in a bag.

**Do:** nothing new. Keep the routine. Keep the bag warm. Count the pantry.

### Week 4: the first mushrooms

**Picture:** small grey-brown shells, like little ears, coming out of the holes.

**Do:**
1. Move the bag to a place with a little light. Spray with water twice a day.
2. Pick them when the edges of the cap start to flatten. Twist and pull.
3. **Always cook them.** Fry them in a little oil with salt.

**Eat:** mushroom and bean soup, rice with fried mushrooms. This is your first food made from something you cannot eat.

**Safety:** only eat mushrooms grown from spawn you know. Never eat wild mushrooms you do not know. One mistake can kill.

### Month 2: the first mealworm harvest

**Picture:** a box of fat, pale worms, each as long as a thumbnail.

**Do:**
1. Pour the bin through a sieve. Keep the **biggest worms** for eating. Keep a **handful of fat worms** aside. They become beetles, then lay the next batch.
2. Leave the ones for eating without food for **one day**.
3. Roast them dry in a pan for 5 to 10 minutes until crunchy. Or grind them to flour and stir a spoon into porridge.

**Eat:** porridge with mealworm flour, rice with roasted worms, mushroom soup.

**Safety:** if you are allergic to shrimp or dust mites, you may react to worms. Try a tiny amount first.

### Month 3: stretch mode

**Picture:** a bucket that is now half empty.

**Do:**
1. Count what is left. Divide by the days you still need. Make the plate smaller if you must, but **never drop the oil to zero**. Fat keeps you warm and your body running.
2. Make every meal a soup. Soup stretches food: the same rice feeds you longer in water.
3. Harvest your first small potatoes if you planted them early.

**Eat:** one mug of dry rice or beans a day, a smaller oil shot, and every day some mushrooms, worms or sprouts.

### Month 4 and on: the cycle

**Picture:** a small cycle that runs by itself.

- Jars every day.
- A new mushroom bag every 3 to 4 weeks.
- A worm harvest every 6 to 8 weeks.
- Potatoes every 3 to 4 months.

If your pantry is empty, this cycle will **not** feed you alone. Calories from jars and mushrooms are small. This is when you need the next step.

## Part 3: What to do when the pantry runs out

1. **More fat and starch, not more sprouts.** Potatoes, sweet potatoes, sunflower seeds, pumpkin seeds.
2. **An animal that eats what you cannot.** A goat eats grass, leaves and brush, and gives milk. Hens eat scraps and worms, and give eggs. Rabbits eat weeds, but their meat is lean, so you also need fat.
3. **Wild starch**, if you know it: acorns (soak in water for days to wash out the bitter part), cattail roots, dandelion roots.
4. **Your neighbours.** Trade your worms, mushrooms, skills and eggs for rice, oil and salt. A group survives longer than a person.

## Five mistakes that hurt people

- **Too little fat.** Rice and sprouts alone make you weak. Keep the oil.
- **Only lean meat or only sprouts.** Your body needs fat, starch and protein together.
- **Dirty water.** Boil it.
- **Eating a mushroom, root or seed you do not know.** Leave it.
- **Running out of fuel before food.** Use the pressure cooker and the hay box.

## One sentence to remember

**The pantry keeps you alive, the jar, the bag and the bin keep you well, and the earth and the animals feed you after.**

*This is a plain-language guide, not medical advice. The calorie numbers are rounded, from standard food tables. Test every step once now, while the shops are open.*
